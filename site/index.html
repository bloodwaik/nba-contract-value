Which contracts give teams a cap-space superpower?

Imagine you sign a rookie to a $13M deal and he plays like a $36M superstar. Your team just got $23M of free cap space to spend on the rest of the roster. That's surplus value — the gap between what a player produces and what their contract costs. This site finds the league's biggest bargains and biggest overpays.

How to read this site
1 · We learn the market

A regression looks at every player's stats and what their contract costs, and learns what one unit of production is worth on the open market.

2 · We price every player

For each player we get an "earned" cap % — what the model says the open market would pay them today.

3 · We subtract

Surplus = Earned − Paid. Positive means the team gets a bargain. Negative means the contract is upside-down.

Cheat sheet

Plain-English definitions of the stats we use
Salary as % of cap

A player's salary divided by the league cap. Lets us compare a $30M deal today vs. a $30M deal in 2019 fairly.

BPM (Box Plus/Minus)

A single number for impact. Above 0 = better than league-average. +5 is All-Star, +8 is MVP tier.

Game Score (GmSc)

A box-score number for a single game. ~10 is a starter's night, 25+ is a star performance.

TS% (True Shooting)

One number that combines 2s, 3s, and free throws. League average is ~58%. 60%+ is elite.

USG% (Usage Rate)

Share of team possessions the player consumes while on the floor. 30%+ = primary star, 15% = role player.

"Earned" vs "Paid"

Paid = actual cap hit. Earned = what the model says the market would pay them. Surplus is the gap.
